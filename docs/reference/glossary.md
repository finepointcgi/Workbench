# Glossary of terms

Plain-English meanings of the government contracting and accounting terms Workbench uses. The same explanations appear in the app when you hover over a word with a dotted underline, and on **Help › Glossary**.

## Costs and charging

**ODC** (Other direct cost)
:   A cost other than labor that's charged straight to a project, such as travel, materials or a vendor's invoice.

**WBS** (Work breakdown structure)
:   A project's outline of tasks. The lowest-level tasks are the charge codes you record time and costs against.

**Charge code**
:   The project task you record your hours or costs against, for example "Requirements review (1001.01.02)".

**Direct** (Direct cost)
:   Work or spending for one specific project or contract, billed to that customer.

**Indirect** (Indirect cost)
:   Work or spending that supports the whole company (training, proposals, admin). It's spread over projects through overhead and G&A rates.

**Labor category**
:   A job title the contract prices and bills by, such as "Senior Engineer" (contracts may call it a PLC or LCAT). The budget counts hours by labor category, and each person bills as one.

**Budget**
:   How many hours of each labor category, and how many dollars of other costs, each WBS element may use. Set it before staffing people.

**Plan**
:   The project's schedule: tasks, dates and who does them, in the plan sheet. Its tasks mirror the WBS.

**Standard hours**
:   The hours you're expected to record in a period (usually 40 a week), less holidays.

**Burdened** (Burdened cost)
:   The full cost: raw labor or expense plus fringe, overhead and G&A added on top.

**Burden**
:   The fringe, overhead and G&A costs added on top of raw labor or expense to get its full cost.

**Fringe**
:   The cost of employee benefits (paid leave, health insurance, payroll taxes), spread over labor as a percentage.

**Overhead**
:   The cost of supporting project work (facilities, supervision, equipment), spread over direct labor as a percentage.

**G&A** (General and administrative)
:   The cost of running the company (executives, accounting, HR), spread over total project cost as a percentage.

**Indirect rate**
:   A percentage (fringe, overhead or G&A) added to direct costs so each project carries its share of company costs.

**Provisional rate** (Provisional billing rate)
:   An indirect rate used for billing during the year, before the actual rate is known. It's trued up to actuals afterwards.

**Unallowable** (Unallowable cost)
:   A cost the government won't pay for under FAR 31.205 (alcohol, entertainment, some travel). It's recorded but kept out of bills and rates.

**Allowable** (Allowable cost)
:   A cost the government accepts as chargeable under FAR 31.2, so it can be billed and included in rates.

**Allocation**
:   Spreading an indirect cost pool over the projects that benefit from it, using the indirect rate.

## Budgets and earned value

**BAC** (Budget at completion)
:   The total approved budget for the work.

**ETC** (Estimate to complete)
:   How much more the work is expected to cost from today until it's finished.

**EAC** (Estimate at completion)
:   What the work is expected to cost in total when it's finished: spent so far plus the estimate to complete.

**VAC** (Variance at completion)
:   Budget at completion minus estimate at completion. Negative means the work is expected to finish over budget.

**EV** (Earned value)
:   The budgeted value of the work actually done so far.

**PV** (Planned value)
:   The budgeted value of the work that was scheduled to be done by now.

**AC** (Actual cost)
:   What the work done so far actually cost.

**CV** (Cost variance)
:   Earned value minus actual cost. Negative means the work done cost more than planned.

**SV** (Schedule variance)
:   Earned value minus planned value. Negative means less work is done than scheduled.

**CPI** (Cost performance index)
:   Earned value ÷ actual cost. 1.0 is on budget; below 1.0 means over budget.

**SPI** (Schedule performance index)
:   Earned value ÷ planned value. 1.0 is on schedule; below 1.0 means behind schedule.

**BCWS** (Budgeted cost of work scheduled)
:   The planned value: the budget for the work that was scheduled to be done by now.

**BCWP** (Budgeted cost of work performed)
:   The earned value: the budget for the work actually done so far.

**ACWP** (Actual cost of work performed)
:   What the work done so far actually cost.

**PMB** (Performance measurement baseline)
:   The time-phased budget that earned value is measured against.

**MR** (Management reserve)
:   Budget held back for unplanned work within the project's scope, not yet assigned to any task.

**VAR** (Variance analysis report)
:   A required explanation of why a control account is over or under its cost or schedule thresholds, and what's being done about it.

**TCPI** (To-complete performance index)
:   How efficient the remaining work must be to finish on budget. Above 1.0 means you have to do better than planned.

**Baseline**
:   The approved, locked plan (budget and schedule) that progress is measured against.

**Control account**
:   A part of the project, usually one WBS branch, where budget, schedule and actual cost are managed and reported together.

**Work package**
:   A lowest-level piece of work with its own budget and schedule; earned value is measured on it.

**Burn** (Burn rate)
:   How fast the budget or funding is being spent.

## Contracts, funding and billing

**CLIN** (Contract line item number)
:   A separately priced line on a government contract. Each one is funded and billed on its own.

**Ceiling**
:   The most the customer will pay: the contract value or a cap on hours, cost or rates. Costs above it can't be billed.

**Funded** (Funded amount)
:   Money the government has actually obligated so far. You can't bill past it, even if the contract value is higher.

**Funding**
:   Money the government has obligated to the contract so far. Work and billing must stay within it.

**Contract value**
:   The total price of the contract if every option is exercised. Usually more than is funded today.

**FFP** (Firm fixed price)
:   A contract paid at a set price no matter what the work costs.

**T&M** (Time and materials)
:   A contract billed by the hour at each labor category's rate, plus materials at cost.

**Cost-plus**
:   A contract that pays allowable costs plus a fee.

**Fee**
:   The profit on a cost-plus contract, on top of allowable cost.

**Fee withhold**
:   Part of the fee the government holds back on cost-plus invoices until the contract is closed out.

**Retainage**
:   Part of each invoice the customer holds back until the work is accepted or the contract closes.

**Unbilled**
:   Work done and costs incurred that haven't been invoiced yet.

**Aging**
:   How long unpaid invoices have been outstanding, grouped by days past the invoice date.

**WAWF** (Wide Area Workflow)
:   The Defense Department's system for submitting invoices and receiving reports.

**Closeout** (Contract closeout)
:   Finishing a contract: final rates, final invoice, releasing withholds and closing the books.

## Revenue and accounting

**Revenue recognition**
:   Deciding how much of the contract's value has been earned this period, by the project's revenue method.

**Percent complete**
:   Revenue earned in proportion to the share of the total estimated cost spent so far.

**Accrual**
:   Recording revenue or cost in the period it was earned or incurred, even if it hasn't been invoiced or paid yet.

**Incurred cost submission**
:   The annual report of actual indirect rates and costs contractors send the DCAA within six months of year-end.

**DCAA** (Defense Contract Audit Agency)
:   The government auditors who review contractors' timekeeping, accounting and billing.

**Journal entry**
:   A record of debits and credits posted to the general ledger.

**Fiscal period**
:   An accounting month. Time and costs post to the period they fall in; closed periods can't change.

## Purchasing and assets

**PO** (Purchase order)
:   The order sent to a vendor that commits the company to buy something at an agreed price.

**Requisition** (Purchase requisition)
:   A request to buy something. Once approved, purchasing turns it into a purchase order.

**Committed** (Committed cost)
:   Money promised on purchase orders that hasn't been billed yet.

**Three-way match**
:   Checking that a vendor's invoice agrees with the purchase order and with what was received before it's paid.

**Match exception** (Three-way match exception)
:   A vendor invoice whose quantity or price doesn't agree with the purchase order or what was received, so it's held until someone resolves it.

**Capitalize**
:   Record a purchase as a fixed asset and spread its cost over its useful life (depreciation) instead of expensing it all at once.

**Depreciation**
:   Spreading a fixed asset's cost over the years it's used.

**Subcontract**
:   Work on your contract done by another company, paid through their invoices.

**Bill #** (Voucher number)
:   The number Workbench gives a vendor bill when it's entered (Costpoint calls it a voucher). The vendor's own number is the Invoice.

**AP** (Accounts payable)
:   Bills from vendors the company owes and hasn't paid yet.

## Resourcing

**Staffed hours**
:   Hours a person is staffed to work on a project in a week, in the labor category they bill as.

**Staffing**
:   Named people's hours a week on a project, in the labor category they bill as. Staffing fills the budget's hours; timesheets record what was actually worked.

**Capacity**
:   The hours a person can work in a week: standard hours less holidays, approved leave and time for non-project work.

**Utilization**
:   The share of a person's available hours spent on billable or direct work.
